What Matters in Multilingual F5-TTS?

Abstract

How far can a widely used non-autoregressive TTS backbone go with better conditioning and alignment? We examine this question through controlled experiments with F5-TTS across 11 Indian languages and Indian English. Our results show that shared phonetic representations and explicit language identity do not independently improve intelligibility over characters; their benefits emerge through complementary alignment supervision and contextual conditioning. The resulting recipe, using mixed character–IPA training with character inference, reduces validation CER by 16% relative to the character baseline. Scaled to 22 Indian languages plus English, it outperforms all three evaluated open-source systems on WER and CER across the 12 primary test languages, improving over SPRING-F5 (WER: 12.71 → 10.54; CER: 3.30 → 2.07). Code-switched evaluation further shows that word-level language modulation improves embedded English-word recovery, while inserted language tokens reduce it. These findings establish an effective multilingual conditioning recipe and show why individual design choices must be evaluated together.
